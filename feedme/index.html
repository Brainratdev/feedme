How Gob digests

pump.fun pays the dev a small creator fee on every trade. Here, that fee never reaches the dev's pocket. It goes straight to Gob.

  1. 1

    You trade

    Buy or sell $FEEDME on pump.fun or PumpSwap. Any trade works. Gob doesn't care which way.

  2. 2

    A fee drops

    The protocol sends the creator fee to the public Stomach Wallet, the only wallet that receives fees.

  3. 3

    It gets chewed

    Half of the SOL buys $FEEDME, and the other half stays as SOL, so the pool gets a balanced bite.

  4. 4

    Swallowed

    Both halves get added to the $FEEDME/SOL pool. The LP tokens are burned, so no one can pull that liquidity back out.

  5. 5

    Gob grows

    A deeper pool means lower price impact on every trade. More trades, more fees, more food. The loop repeats.

Egg phase:Before graduating from the bonding curve there's no pool to feed yet. Fees wait in the Stomach Wallet (you can check it any time), and the whole stash gets fed in once Gob hatches on PumpSwap.

Questions from the crowd

Does feeding the pool make the price go up?

Not directly. A deeper pool means each trade moves the price less, so it's harder to dump hard and easier to buy big. The step that buys $FEEDME with half the fee does add a little buy pressure. Nothing guarantees number go up. Gob is a monster, not a promise.

Can the dev just stop feeding it?

Technically, yes. pump.fun doesn't force creator fees anywhere, which is exactly why every meal is posted with its tx hash and the Stomach Wallet is public. If the log goes quiet while volume is up, call it out.

Why burn the LP tokens?

Burned LP means the liquidity that was fed in can never be withdrawn by anyone, dev included. Food, once swallowed, stays swallowed.

How often does Gob eat?

The dev claims fees and feeds the pool on a regular schedule (see the log). Tiny amounts wait until they're worth the transaction fees, because Gob hates wasting gas.

What happens after Kaiju?

Nobody knows. Nobody has fed a Gob that much. Its mouth is already charging something.

Is this financial advice?

No. It's a hungry cartoon. Memecoins are extremely risky, so only put in what you're fine losing to a monster.
